# **Product Requirements Document: Unified Notification & Signaling (UNS) System for Autonomous Agent Governance**

## **1\. Introduction and Strategic Vision**

### **1.1 Executive Summary**

The financial services sector stands at a precipice of transformation driven by the deployment of autonomous AI agents. Unlike traditional algorithmic trading systems, which execute deterministic logic within strictly defined parameters, agentic AI possesses the capability to reason, plan, and execute multi-step workflows with a degree of autonomy that current governance frameworks are ill-equipped to manage. The introduction of these non-deterministic actors into high-stakes environments—such as High-Frequency Trading (HFT), post-trade settlement, and regulatory reporting—creates a profound "Compliance-Autonomy Paradox." Institutions must harness the velocity and adaptability of AI agents while simultaneously enforcing the rigid, deterministic controls mandated by global regulators.

The Unified Notification & Signaling (UNS) system is proposed as the definitive infrastructure solution to this paradox. It is not merely a message bus or a logging tool; it is a comprehensive **Governance Control Plane**. The UNS is architected to intercept, evaluate, and orchestrate every critical decision made by an autonomous agent, enforcing a rigorous "Human-in-the-Loop" (HITL) lifecycle where necessary, and guaranteeing cryptographic non-repudiation for all actions. By imposing a state-machine-driven architecture on agent communications, the UNS transforms the "Black Box" of AI cognition into a transparent, audit-grade ledger that satisfies the stringent requirements of the EU’s Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and the US Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA).1

### **1.2 Product Vision and Value Proposition**

The vision for the UNS is to serve as the "Central Nervous System" for the regulated autonomous enterprise. It acts as the immutable arbiter of truth between the high-velocity world of autonomous agents and the high-liability world of financial compliance.

The core value proposition rests on three pillars:

1. **Latency-Aware Governance:** The system distinguishes between the microsecond operational tempo of execution agents and the human-speed cadence of strategic oversight. It provides a tiered architecture that applies automated, ultra-low-latency risk checks to HFT flows while routing strategic decisions through durable, asynchronous human approval workflows.3
2. **Regulatory Immunity by Design:** By embedding compliance logic directly into the signaling layer, the UNS ensures that no agent can act without a valid, policy-compliant signal. It automates the generation of FINRA Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT) reports and DORA incident notifications, drastically reducing the risk of regulatory fines and reputational damage.5
3. **Operational Safety and Resilience:** The UNS integrates advanced "Kill Switch" and "Circuit Breaker" patterns to detect and neutralize runaway agent behaviors—such as infinite loops or hallucinated strategies—before they manifest as market risk. It provides a "Shadow Mode" for safe agent training and validation, ensuring that new models can be tested against live market data without financial exposure.7

### **1.3 Scope and Target Audience**

This Product Requirements Document (PRD) defines the functional and non-functional requirements for the UNS v1.0.

**Primary Stakeholders:**

* **Chief Compliance Officers (CCOs) & Risk Managers:** Require absolute visibility into agent decision-making processes and the ability to demonstrate "effective supervision" under FINRA Rule 3110\.9
* **Head of Algorithmic Trading:** Needs to deploy increasingly sophisticated AI strategies without incurring unacceptable latency penalties or operational fragility.
* **DevOps & SRE Teams:** Responsible for the reliability of the agent infrastructure and the implementation of "break glass" procedures during critical incidents.
* **Regulatory Bodies (External):** Entities such as the SEC, FINRA, and European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs) who are the ultimate consumers of the audit trails generated by the UNS.

**2\. Regulatory and Compliance Landscape**

The architectural mandates of the UNS are derived directly from a complex web of global financial regulations. The system must not only comply with these rules but must also automate the evidence of compliance.

### **2.1 The Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)**

The EU's DORA framework fundamentally shifts the regulatory focus from financial resilience to digital operational resilience. It mandates that financial entities must be able to withstand, respond to, and recover from ICT-related disruptions.10

**Key Requirements for UNS:**

* **Incident Reporting Timelines (Article 20):** DORA imposes strict deadlines for reporting "major" ICT-related incidents. The UNS must automatically classify agent-induced incidents and generate an "Initial Notification" to the competent authority within **4 hours** of classification.6 This requires the UNS to have real-time anomaly detection capabilities that can distinguish between a benign agent error and a systemic failure.
* **Third-Party Risk Management (Article 28):** Financial entities are responsible for the risks posed by their ICT third-party service providers. Since many agents rely on external LLMs (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic), the UNS must log and monitor all external API calls to track concentration risk and dependency health in real-time.6
* **Resilience Testing:** The UNS must support "Digital Operational Resilience Testing" by enabling controlled fault injection and "Shadow Mode" simulations to verify the stability of agent fleets under stress.1

### **2.2 FINRA and the Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT)**

In the United States, FINRA and the SEC enforce strict rules regarding market transparency and supervision.

**Key Requirements for UNS:**

* **Consolidated Audit Trail (Rule 613):** The UNS must facilitate the reporting of every order event (receipt, routing, modification, cancellation, execution) to the CAT. Crucially, this includes precise timestamping. The UNS must ensure that all agent clocks are synchronized to within **50 milliseconds** of NIST time (and potentially tighter for HFT), logging any drift.2
* **Supervision of Algorithmic Trading (Rule 3110):** Firms must have a supervisory system reasonably designed to achieve compliance with securities laws. For "Black Box" agents, this means recording the "Chain of Thought" (CoT)—the reasoning trace that led to a trade decision. The UNS must capture this reasoning alongside the trade instruction to prove that the agent's actions were consistent with its design parameters and risk limits.9
* **Market Access (Rule 15c3-5):** This rule requires pre-trade risk controls to prevent erroneous orders. The UNS must integrate with risk engines to provide pre-trade blocks that operate with sub-millisecond latency, acting as a "hard stop" for rogue agents.14

### **2.3 AI Governance Frameworks**

Emerging standards like the EU AI Act, NIST AI Risk Management Framework (RMF), and ISO 42001 emphasize the need for transparency, accountability, and human oversight in AI systems.

**Key Requirements for UNS:**

* **Human Oversight:** High-risk AI systems (which likely include autonomous trading agents) must be designed to allow for effective human oversight. The UNS implements this via the "Human-in-the-Loop" signaling mechanism, ensuring that humans can intervene, interrupt, or override agent actions.15
* **Explainability:** Governance requires that agent decisions be explainable. The UNS’s immutable audit log serves as the repository for these explanations, linking model inputs, prompts, and outputs to real-world actions.17

**3\. High-Level System Architecture**

The UNS employs a sophisticated Event-Driven Architecture (EDA) to decouple governance logic from agent business logic. This separation of concerns is critical to maintaining system agility while enforcing rigid controls.

### **3.1 Architectural Pattern: The Governance Sidecar**

To ensure universal coverage across heterogenous agent frameworks (LangChain, AutoGen, proprietary Python scripts), the UNS utilizes a **Sidecar Pattern**. A lightweight governance proxy is deployed alongside every agent container.

* **Interception:** The sidecar intercepts all outbound network traffic from the agent, including API calls to exchanges, LLM providers, and internal tools.
* **Policy Enforcement:** Before any request leaves the agent's environment, the sidecar evaluates it against a local cache of governance policies (defined in ODRL).
* **Signaling:** If the action requires approval or logging, the sidecar handles the communication with the central UNS Control Plane, keeping the agent logic simple and focused on its task.

This approach ensures "Governance by Default." Even if an agent developer forgets to implement logging code, the infrastructure guarantees that no unapproved action can escape the container.19

### **3.2 The Latency Tiering Model**

Recognizing the diverse speed requirements of financial operations, the UNS architecture is stratified into three distinct latency tiers.

| Latency Tier | Use Case | Implementation Strategy | Target Latency | Governance Mechanism | Source |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Tier 1: Ultra-Low Latency** | Pre-trade risk checks, HFT execution orders, "Kill Switch" activation. | FPGA-based filtering or kernel-level eBPF interception. In-memory policy evaluation. | \< 100 µs | Automated "Hard Blocks" (Rule 15c3-5). No human intervention possible. | 4 |
| **Tier 2: Low Latency** | Algorithmic strategy adjustments, quote updates, intra-day rebalancing. | High-performance message bus (Solace/Kafka). Sidecar-based policy checks. | \< 50 ms | Automated policy validation against ODRL rules. Async logging. | 3 |
| **Tier 3: Asynchronous** | Strategic portfolio reallocations, deploying new agent models, large block trades. | Durable Workflow Orchestrator (Temporal). Human-in-the-Loop approval workflows. | Minutes to Hours | Cryptographically signed human authorization. Deep reasoning analysis. | 23 |

### **3.3 Core Components**

The UNS ecosystem comprises four primary subsystems:

1. **The Governance Control Plane:** The central brain that manages policy definitions, agent identities, and the global state of the system.
2. **The Signal Router:** A high-throughput, fault-tolerant message broker responsible for delivering signals between agents, humans, and regulators.
3. **The State Machine Orchestrator:** A durable execution engine that manages long-running workflows, such as multi-stage human approvals or complex incident reporting sequences.
4. **The Immutable Ledger:** A cryptographically secured storage layer for audit trails, ensuring non-repudiation and tamper evidence.

**4\. The Governance Control Plane (Policy & State)**

The Governance Control Plane is the administrative heart of the UNS. It translates high-level regulatory requirements and internal risk mandates into executable technical policies.

### **4.1 Policy Definition with ODRL**

To ensure policies are machine-readable, interoperable, and rigorous, the UNS adopts the **Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL)** W3C standard. ODRL allows for the granular expression of permissions, prohibitions, and obligations.25

**Example Policy Scenario:**

An agent is permitted to trade equities but prohibited from short-selling restricted stocks, and must obtain human approval for any trade exceeding $1 million.

**ODRL Implementation:**

JSON

{
  "@context": "http://www.w3.org/ns/odrl.jsonld",
  "@type": "Policy",
  "uid": "http://example.com/policy:101",
  "permission": \[{
    "target": "asset:equity:\*",
    "action": "trade:buy",
    "assigner": "compliance-officer",
    "assignee": "agent:trading-bot-v1",
    "constraint": \[{
      "leftOperand": "transactionAmount",
      "operator": "lteq",
      "rightOperand": "1000000"
    }\]
  }\],
  "prohibition": \[{
    "target": "asset:equity:restricted\_list",
    "action": "trade:short"
  }\],
  "obligation": \[{
    "action": "log:audit\_trail",
    "target": "system:audit\_ledger"
  }\]
}

The Governance Gateway parses these ODRL policies and enforces them at the edge (in the sidecar). This ensures that policy decisions are made as close to the agent as possible, minimizing latency.20

### **4.2 The Governance State Machine**

Every significant agent action triggers a transition in the Governance State Machine. This FSM is not ephemeral; it is managed by a durable orchestrator (like Temporal) to ensure that the state is preserved even in the event of a total system crash.27

**State Transitions:**

* CREATED: The agent has formulated an intent (e.g., "I want to buy AAPL"). A signal is emitted to the UNS.
* PENDING\_POLICY: The UNS validates the intent against ODRL policies.
  * *Transition:* If policy passes and amount \< threshold \-\> EXECUTING.
  * *Transition:* If policy passes but amount \> threshold \-\> PENDING\_APPROVAL.
  * *Transition:* If policy fails \-\> REJECTED.
* PENDING\_APPROVAL: The agent is suspended. The system awaits a cryptographic signal from a human.
  * *Transition:* Human signs approval \-\> APPROVED.
  * *Transition:* Human rejects or SLA timeout \-\> REJECTED.
* EXECUTING: The agent is permitted to proceed. The sidecar releases the network block.
* COMPLETED: The exchange acknowledges the trade.
* TERMINATED: A "Kill Switch" event forced the immediate cessation of the workflow.

This state machine provides the rigorous "Chain of Custody" required by DORA. At any moment, compliance officers can query the exact state of any transaction.29

### **4.3 Kill Switches and Circuit Breakers**

In an era of autonomous agents, the ability to stop a system is as important as the ability to start it. The UNS implements a tiered "Emergency Brake" system.

**Circuit Breakers (Automated):**

Agents can enter failure modes such as infinite loops (repeatedly trying to buy a stock with insufficient funds) or hallucination loops (repeatedly calling a tool with invalid arguments). The UNS monitors the *velocity* and *error rate* of signals from each agent.

* **Requirement:** If an agent exceeds ![][image1] errors per second or ![][image2] identical requests, the circuit breaker trips, automatically transitioning the agent to a SUSPENDED state and alerting operators.7

**Global Kill Switch (Manual):**

In the event of a catastrophic market event or systemic algorithm failure, risk managers need a "Panic Button."

* **Requirement:** The UNS Dashboard must provide a Global Kill Switch that, when activated, instantly revokes the API credentials for all agents (or a specific subset) and instructs the sidecars to drop all outbound traffic. This action must be propagated via a high-priority control channel to ensure it takes effect within milliseconds.19

**5\. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Signaling & Orchestration**

The "Human-in-the-Loop" is the central mechanism for reconciling AI autonomy with regulatory accountability. However, implementing HITL in high-speed systems presents significant technical challenges regarding state management and resource utilization.

### **5.1 The "Interrupt" Pattern & Durable Execution**

Traditional synchronous API calls cannot support HITL workflows because human response times are unpredictable (minutes to hours). Blocking a thread for that long is resource-inefficient and fragile. The UNS employs the **"Interrupt" pattern** (as seen in LangGraph and Temporal).32

**Workflow Mechanics:**

1. **Checkpointing:** When an agent encounters a PENDING\_APPROVAL state, the UNS triggers a checkpoint. The agent's entire memory state (variables, context window, execution stack) is serialized and persisted to the database.
2. **Suspension:** The compute resources associated with the agent (e.g., the GPU/CPU container) are released. The agent effectively "sleeps" without consuming expensive infrastructure.
3. **Signaling:** The UNS generates a secure notification (via Slack, Email, or Mobile App) to the designated human approver.
4. **Resumption:** When the human approves, the UNS retrieves the serialized checkpoint, rehydrates the agent's state, and resumes execution on an available worker. To the agent, it appears as if the function call simply took a long time to return.

This architecture ensures that the system is **Resilient** (approvals survive server restarts) and **Cost-Effective** (idle agents don't burn GPU time).23

### **5.2 Cryptographic Authorization (JWS)**

A simple "Click to Approve" button is insufficient for financial non-repudiation. If a trade goes wrong, the approver might claim they never clicked it, or that the system was hacked.

**Requirement:** All human approvals must be cryptographically signed.

* **Mechanism:** When a human clicks "Approve," the UNS Dashboard triggers a client-side signing event. The approval payload (containing the transaction ID, timestamp, and details) is signed using the user's private key (stored in a secure hardware token or WebAuthn device).
* **Format:** The resulting signature is formatted as a **JSON Web Signature (JWS)**.35
* **Verification:** The UNS server verifies the JWS against the user's public key before transitioning the state to APPROVED. This creates an undeniable mathematical proof of who authorized the action and exactly what they authorized.37

### **5.3 Asynchronous Approval Workflow UX**

The interaction model for humans must be designed to minimize friction while maximizing context.

**The "Control Tower" Dashboard:**

* **Contextual Presentation:** The approver is not just shown a raw JSON blob. The dashboard renders the agent's "Chain of Thought" (why it wants to trade), the relevant market data (price, volume), and the risk assessment score.
* **Granular Approval:** The approver can modify the parameters (e.g., "Approved, but limit price to $150") before signing.
* **Batch Operations:** For lower-risk items, the interface supports batch signing to prevent approval fatigue.

**6\. Agent Integration & The Model Context Protocol (MCP)**

To ensure widespread adoption, the UNS must integrate seamlessly with the diverse ecosystem of AI agent frameworks. The UNS leverages the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** as the standard interface for agent-tool communication.

### **6.1 UNS as an MCP Server**

The UNS exposes its governance primitives as an MCP Server. Agents (acting as MCP Clients) connect to the UNS to access "Governance Tools".39

**Exposed MCP Tools:**

1. ask\_for\_approval(context, action\_details): The agent calls this tool when it identifies a high-stakes decision. This tool triggers the HITL workflow described above.
2. log\_reasoning(trace\_id, content): Allows the agent to explicitly log its internal monologue or reasoning steps to the immutable ledger.
3. check\_policy(action): Allows the agent to pre-validate an action against ODRL policies before attempting to execute it, avoiding unnecessary rejections.

**Integration Benefits:**

* **Standardization:** Any agent framework that supports MCP (which is becoming the industry standard) can integrate with the UNS without custom SDKs.
* **Portability:** Agents can be moved between different environments (dev, test, prod) without changing their code; only the MCP server configuration changes.

### **6.2 Legacy Integration via Sidecars**

For legacy algorithmic trading bots or agents that do not support MCP, the UNS relies on the **Network Sidecar** approach.

* The sidecar (e.g., an Envoy proxy) is configured to inspect outbound traffic.
* It identifies specific patterns (e.g., FIX messages, REST calls to exchange APIs).
* It automatically wraps these calls in UNS governance events, effectively "retrofitting" governance onto legacy systems.19

**7\. Data Models, Schemas & Audit Trails**

The integrity of the UNS relies on rigorous data modeling. Loose schemas lead to "garbage in, garbage out," which is unacceptable for regulatory reporting.

### **7.1 The Unified Event Schema**

All events within the UNS—whether from agents, humans, or system monitors—conform to a strict JSON schema based on the **CloudEvents** specification.42

**Schema Structure:**

JSON

{
  "specversion": "1.0",
  "id": "evt-uuid-v4",
  "source": "agent:strategy-alpha-v2",
  "type": "com.fintech.uns.governance.request",
  "time": "2026-01-22T16:00:00.123Z",
  "datacontenttype": "application/json",
  "data": {
    "intent": "EXECUTE\_TRADE",
    "parameters": {
      "symbol": "AAPL",
      "side": "BUY",
      "quantity": 1000,
      "order\_type": "LIMIT",
      "limit\_price": 150.25
    },
    "reasoning\_trace": {
      "model": "gpt-4-turbo",
      "prompt\_hash": "sha256:...",
      "rationale": "Moving average crossover detected..."
    },
    "risk\_metrics": {
      "portfolio\_exposure": 0.12,
      "var\_contribution": 0.05
    }
  },
  "extensions": {
    "traceparent": "00-4bf92f3577b34da6a3ce929d0e0e4736-00f067aa0ba902b7-01",
    "finra\_cat\_id": "CAT-20260122-XYZ",
    "dora\_classification": "critical"
  }
}

This schema ensures that every event carries the necessary metadata for tracing (OpenTelemetry traceparent), regulatory reporting (finra\_cat\_id), and risk classification.43

### **7.2 Immutable Audit Ledger (Merkle Trees)**

To prevent tampering, audit logs are not just stored in a database; they are secured in a cryptographic ledger.

* **Merkle Tree Structure:** Events are hashed and organized into a Merkle Tree. The root hash of the tree is periodically published to a separate, immutable medium (e.g., a WORM blockchain or a public ledger).
* **Verification:** Auditors can cryptographically verify that a specific log entry exists in the tree and has not been altered since its creation. This provides the mathematical certainty required for forensic investigations.38

### **7.3 Clock Synchronization Strategy**

To meet FINRA Rule 6800 requirements (50ms drift tolerance), the UNS implements a robust timekeeping architecture.

* **PTP (Precision Time Protocol):** The server infrastructure utilizes PTP (IEEE 1588\) for microsecond-level synchronization across the local network.
* **Drift Monitoring:** The sidecar continuously monitors the agent container's clock against the PTP master. If drift exceeds a safety threshold (e.g., 20ms), the sidecar blocks all trading actions and alerts operations.2

**8\. Operational Resilience, Security & "Break Glass"**

### **8.1 Identity Management (SPIFFE/SPIRE)**

In a containerized environment, static API keys are a security liability. The UNS utilizes the **Secure Production Identity Framework for Everyone (SPIFFE)**.

* **Workload Identity:** Every agent container is issued a short-lived, cryptographically verifiable identity document (SVID) by the SPIRE server upon startup.
* **mTLS:** Agents use this SVID to establish mutual TLS (mTLS) connections with the UNS. This ensures that the UNS knows exactly *which* code is running and that it hasn't been tampered with.
* **Attestation:** SPIRE performs node attestation to verify the underlying host integrity before issuing identities.45

### **8.2 Hallucination Detection & Grounding**

A key operational risk is an agent hallucinating market data or misinterpreting a policy. The UNS includes a **Grounding Layer**.

* **RAG Validation:** When an agent asserts a fact (e.g., "The price of AAPL is $150"), the UNS performs a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) check against an authoritative source (e.g., a direct exchange feed) to validate the claim.
* **Consistency Checks:** If the agent's internal reasoning contradicts the market reality (e.g., buying based on a price that doesn't exist), the request is flagged as a "Hallucination" and blocked.46

### **8.3 "Break Glass" Procedures**

In emergency scenarios (e.g., the UNS governance engine is malfunctioning and blocking legitimate closing trades), human operators need a bypass mechanism.

* **Dual-Key Authorization:** Activating "Break Glass" mode requires digital signatures from two distinct authorized senior administrators (the "Two-Man Rule").
* **Enhanced Logging:** When in Break Glass mode, logging verbosity is increased to the maximum level, and alerts are broadcast to the entire compliance team.
* **Post-Mortem:** The system automatically forces a post-mortem workflow once normal operations resume, requiring documentation of why the bypass was necessary.48

**9\. Deployment Strategy: From Shadow Mode to Live**

Deploying autonomous agents is inherently risky. The UNS supports a progressive deployment lifecycle to mitigate this risk.

### **9.1 Phase 1: Shadow Mode (The "Dark Launch")**

Before an agent is allowed to trade real capital, it is deployed in **Shadow Mode**.

* **Mechanism:** The agent receives live market data and makes decisions. It sends these decisions to the UNS.
* **Simulation:** The UNS logs the decision and simulates the execution (paper trading) without sending the order to the exchange.
* **Validation:** The UNS compares the agent's "shadow" performance against the live production strategies. Governance policies are evaluated, and any "would-be" violations are logged as critical warnings.
* **Criteria:** An agent must run in Shadow Mode for a configurable period (e.g., 30 days) without a critical policy violation before promotion.8

### **9.2 Phase 2: Canary Deployment & Throttling**

Once promoted, the agent enters the **Canary Phase**.

* **Throttling:** The UNS enforces strict rate limits (e.g., max 10 trades/day) and capital limits (e.g., max $10k exposure).
* **Enhanced Monitoring:** The agent is subject to 100% human review for all actions (all actions trigger PENDING\_APPROVAL).
* **Gradual Release:** As confidence grows, the throttling limits are programmatically relaxed, and the review requirement is shifted to random sampling.

**10\. Conclusion**

The Unified Notification & Signaling (UNS) system is not merely a technical utility; it is a strategic enabler for the future of finance. By solving the Compliance-Autonomy Paradox, it allows financial institutions to unlock the immense potential of agentic AI—speed, adaptability, and efficiency—without compromising on the safety, transparency, and accountability that the global financial system demands.

Through its rigorous adherence to DORA and FINRA standards, its robust HITL orchestration, and its immutable cryptographic audit trails, the UNS transforms the "Black Box" of AI into a "Glass Box." It provides the confidence required to move from experimental AI pilots to enterprise-scale autonomous operations, securing a competitive advantage in the next generation of algorithmic trading.
